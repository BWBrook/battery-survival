![How much observation time is lost between visits, as the revisit interval lengthens, for five white-flash battery scenarios. Lines and shading are posterior means and 95% intervals of expected lost camera-days per cycle from the Weibull camera-effect model; the loglogistic model gives nearly identical curves (Fig. S5). Grey diagonal lines mark losses equal to 5% and 10% of the interval: where a curve crosses a line is the longest revisit interval at that tolerance (Table 2). Each curve stops at the maximum follow-up in its joint brand/flash/region/season cell (open circle). Faded segments have fewer than 20 intervals or 10 cameras at risk; maximum follow-up alone does not establish adequate support. Dotted lines mark the 90- and 120-day intervals used for the cost comparison (Table 3). Each scenario combines a battery with its most frequent region and season and a typical observed workload in that cell (Table S5), so the curves compare observed deployments rather than effects of switching batteries at one site. Lost time is modelled under the last-image-as-failure convention, not measured inactivity.](../../outputs/management/2026-10-18/lost_day_curves/lost_day_curves.png){#fig-lost-day-curves width=90%}
