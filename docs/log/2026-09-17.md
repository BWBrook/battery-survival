# Bounded model and camera-dependence sensitivity analysis

## Authority and endpoint

Barry authorised the bounded model and camera-dependence sensitivity analysis
after answering the questions in correspondence thread 23. Service dates are
treated as unavailable. For cameras found inactive, the last dated image is
minimum confirmed runtime and remains the event-time proxy. A service date
could provide an upper bound on an unobserved interval, but would not identify
the shutdown time. No endpoint reconstruction, new exclusions or new field
explanations were introduced. Suggested SW differences remain speculation.

The specification was written before inspecting these new fits in
`docs/sensitivity_analysis_plan.md`; the candidate set had already been informed
by Fable's exploratory work. This is a bounded sensitivity analysis, not a new
confirmatory validation. Source, pipeline outputs and the manuscript backbone
from September 12 remain intact.

## Comparisons and verified findings

The exact canonical training cohort was retained: 3,216 intervals, 489 recorded
camera IDs, 1,735 events and 1,481 right-censored records. The missing flash
count retains the pipeline's zero-replacement assumption. There are 428 camera
IDs with multiple brands, contributing 3,027 intervals; 99 IDs span multiple
regions. Persistent effects are therefore effects associated with recorded
camera identity, not identified hardware age or site mechanisms.

The original Weibull AIC was reproduced as 18,763.2292104347. In the 12-model
grid, loglogistic with brand-specific scale and region had AIC 18,718.727;
the matched Weibull had AIC 18,729.975. The differences are 44.502 from the
original baseline and 11.248 between these flexible specifications. This
comparison uses the independent-row likelihood, not a cluster-adjusted model
selection criterion.

No no-region family adequately reproduced SW survival at 180 days. For the
183 overlapping Varta/white records, observed KM survival was 0.6011; fitted
values ranged from 0.2894 (original Weibull) to 0.4005 (brand-scale lognormal).
SW was not pooled into training. Region-adjusted camera models were not given
an invented SW coefficient and were not presented as new SW validation.

Four matched Bayesian models used brand-specific AFT scales and region effects:
Weibull and loglogistic, each with/without a normal camera random intercept.
Priors, fixed effects and sampling settings were matched within each pair.
Direct CmdStan made the loglogistic comparison possible without changing family
because of a brms interface limitation. The Stan likelihoods at the frequentist
MLEs agreed to less than 1.3e-11 log-likelihood units.

The median camera log-time SD was 0.1305 under Weibull and 0.1299 under
loglogistic, corresponding to a roughly 1.14 multiplier per SD. These are
estimates of persistent heterogeneity, not causal hardware differences.
All four fits passed the population-parameter diagnostic gate: maximum R-hat
below 1.0022, minimum bulk ESS above 2,285, minimum tail ESS above 3,604,
zero divergences and tree-depth hits, and minimum E-BFMI 0.552. Maximum latent
camera R-hat was below 1.0045; latent diagnostics were also retained.

## Execution and verification

### Servicing and manuscript implications

The matched camera comparison does not substantially change the shorter,
better-supported conditional intervals. Across finite comparisons without a
majority thin-support warning in either regional prediction, the largest
camera-versus-independent median shift was 2.575 days (Weibull,
Ansmann/white, r=50). This is a statement about this scenario set under the
stated descriptive support rule, not general transportability or a universal
three-day bound. For the white-flash r=20/50 scenarios, 95% interval widths
changed by factors of approximately 0.92--1.34: uncertainty often increased,
but not uniformly.

For Energizer/white at r=50, the camera-model optima were 178.7 days
(95% interval 173.1--184.1) under Weibull and 177.6 (172.0--183.6) under
loglogistic. For Varta/white at r=100, they were 186.0 (180.3--192.1) and
195.8 (189.2--202.8); only 12 and 9 matching regional intervals remained at
the respective medians. That roughly ten-day difference is numerically real
but rests on thin support. Fable's general claim of at most three days within
maximum follow-up is therefore not adopted.

Family sensitivity is not confined to times after the last observation.
For Energizer/infrared at r=50, the camera-model medians differed by 8.32 days
(245.6 versus 237.2). The matched flexible frequentist models differed by
about 10.12 days. This small stratum has 25 intervals and eight events; exactly
20 regional intervals remain at these medians. Crossing the descriptive
20-interval threshold does not establish precise within-stratum identification.
The report and decision figure include infrared profiles so this result is not
hidden by a white-flash-only summary.

Camera heterogeneity does not repair the late training tail or establish new
region transfer. At 270 days, Energizer KM survival is 0.405 with 19 intervals
at risk, versus population-marginal camera-model survival of 0.120 (Weibull)
and 0.185 (loglogistic). These are fitted-data diagnostics under the existing
endpoint and censoring conventions; neither family should be treated as a
validated extrapolator.

Long extrapolations can change qualitatively. For Energizer/white at r=200,
the Weibull posterior probability of no finite optimum changes from 0.658
without camera effects to 0.258 with them; its posterior median changes from
Inf to 365.9 days. Both conclusions lie outside the available white-flash
follow-up. They illustrate why no-finite-optimum and finite-but-unsupported
must be distinguished, not evidence for a field schedule.

**Recommendation:** retain camera dependence when presenting uncertainty,
revise the distribution-selection narrative to acknowledge brand-scale and
region sensitivity, and present servicing results as conditional decision
curves with explicit empirical support. Keep the endpoint and SW limitations
visible. Within-model credible intervals do not include family-selection or
unknown endpoint-gap uncertainty, and are not individual-battery prediction
intervals. This tranche is complete; the next task is manuscript integration,
not another open-ended refitting exercise or service-date reconstruction.

### Verification outcome

All 12 frequentist fits converged without warnings. All four Bayesian models
passed the frozen gates. Doubling normal quadrature from 30 to 60 nodes changed
survival at 180 days by at most 2.28e-9 and the checked optimum times by at most
1.91e-6 days, with no finite-optimum classification disagreements. The complete
remote suite passed **182 expectations, zero failures/warnings/skips**, through
`R_PROFILE_USER=/dev/null R_LIBS_USER=<isolated-library> just test`.
An independent methodological review checked the likelihood, marginalisation,
root calculation, support classifications and final interpretation. The two
generated scientific figures were visually inspected.
