# Targeted literature revision

This note records four DOI-verified additions for the manuscript bibliography. The
papers are evidence for specific claims; they do not replace a complete library
reconciliation or justify broad claims about all camera-trap studies.

| Source | Claim it supports | Boundary for use |
|---|---|---|
| Cardona et al. (2025), DOI [10.1007/s10531-025-03153-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10531-025-03153-3) | A Tasmanian camera-trap network can be analysed with survival methods when the event is only known between service visits; the paper uses interval-censored camera theft observations and a Bayesian survival analysis. | Theft is the event, not battery failure. Use it as the closest camera-trap service-window analogue for censoring and model framing, rather than as evidence about battery longevity. |
| Kühl et al. (2023), DOI [10.1002/ece3.10599](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.10599) | Camera-trap survey duration can exceed effective observation time because recovery and retrigger delays create periods in which animals are not photographed; technical timing therefore affects realised survey effort. | The paper addresses distance sampling and detection effort, not battery survival. Use it to motivate effective observation time and avoid treating deployment duration as recorded effort. |
| Newey et al. (2015), DOI [10.1007/s13280-015-0713-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13280-015-0713-1) | Field camera studies can experience operational, metadata, false-positive and false-negative problems; false positives can consume storage and battery capacity and equipment performance is context dependent. | This is a practitioner case study and survey, not a controlled battery comparison. Keep claims contextual and avoid generalising its failure rates to the present camera network. |
| Meek et al. (2014), DOI [10.1007/s10531-014-0712-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10531-014-0712-8) | Camera-trap reports should identify camera models, deployment, settings, study design, coding and analysis details so that results can be compared and replicated. | This is reporting guidance, not evidence for a survival effect. Use it to support the field-protocol and metadata checklist. |

The existing deep-research reports remain useful leads, but the publisher records
and DOI links above are the verification basis for these entries. The manuscript
should cite Cardona only for analogous service-window censoring, Kühl for effective
survey effort, Newey for operational limitations, and Meek for reporting practice.
