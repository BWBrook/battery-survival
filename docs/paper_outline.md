## Working Abstract:

1. Camera trap networks are a well established non-invasive tool for monitoring and studying wildlife. Studies involving camera traps can span months or years of time [User Comment: Most are short-term, but there is an increasing interest in permanent deployments, with all the benefits and challenges these entail.] and involve traps located in geographic regions which can be costly to access. Standard camera traps operate using battery power and knowledge about the longevity and survival characteristics of different batteries can guide servicing decisions.

2. We gathered battery condition data from 3257 camera service events across 835 cameras [User Comment: out-of-date, to update]. The cameras [User comment: A single standardised model taking 8 × AA cells with a modular exchangeable flash unit.] were supplied with seven different brands of batteries ranging from cheap alkaline to expensive lithium. Run times prior to censoring or failure ranged from one month to one year. The cameras have one of two flash types and are located across six regions in Tasmania. A fifth of the cameras are located in the remote south west region of Tasmania and data from these cameras was largely uncensored. In contrast, the cameras in the North East region are serviced regularly [User comment: Might be best to leave such specific detail to the main paper rather than the abstract, it’s a lesser point.]. The remaining four regions (South East, North West, Central, West) comprise what we refer to as the TAS-CAM network and provide the most varied battery data for analysis. We use a non-parametric Kaplan-Meier estimator and a semi-parametric Cox proportional hazards model to examine covariates that effect failure time. Additionally we fit a number of parametric models to the data, assess the ability of estimated parameters to reproduce plausible data and compare model performance. We use this parametric modelling framework to show the relationship between service interval periods, cost and inactive operation time for cameras [User Comment: And to permit model-based extrapolations of service life under different conditions, for predictive planning.].
